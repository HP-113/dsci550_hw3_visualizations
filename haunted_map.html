Description

This U.S. city-level bubble map visualizes the geographic distribution of haunted places, with each bubble representing a city. The size of the bubble correlates with the number of haunted locations reported in that area, and the color gradient—from red to green—indicates relative frequency, with green denoting high haunting density. The visualization was built using a GeoJSON layer combined with processed location data cleaned in Assignment 2 and enriched with disambiguation support from GeoParser.

Insight

Geographic clusters of hauntings emerge clearly in regions such as New England, the Deep South, and Southern California. Cities like New Orleans, Boston, Salem, and San Diego show high frequencies of haunting reports, supporting hypotheses from Assignment 1 that tie ghost activity to population density, historical trauma, and cultural preservation. The Northeast cluster also aligns with early colonial settlements and witch trial history, while the South reflects haunted plantation narratives and Civil War legacies. Meanwhile, urban centers in the West show spikes possibly linked to ghost tourism or media depictions.
